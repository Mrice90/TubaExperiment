# INFINITE CONQUEST — World Bible

**Purpose.** This is the lore consistency contract for Infinite Conquest.
Every card name, description, art brief, trailer script, and campaign beat
must be checkable against this document. If a new card contradicts it, the
card changes — not the bible. (Mechanical design contracts live in
`docs/faction-themes.md`; this document is the story those mechanics tell.)

**Tone.** Neo-futurist Greco-tech. The gods are not marble statues; they are
sovereign intelligences running storm grids, drowned arcologies, war foundries,
and data courts. Ancient names, future machines. Never classical painting.

---

## 1. The World: The Shattered Pantheon

Long ago the world was ruled by the **Titans** — primordial world-engines the
old stories call gods, but who were closer to living infrastructure: weather
systems with wills, ocean currents with appetites, mountains that decided
where cities could stand. They did not govern. They *operated*, and mortals
and young gods alike lived inside their processes.

Then came the **Titanomachy** — the previous war, the one every faction's
history starts from. The young Olympians united: storm against sky-engines,
sea against abyssal engines, war against the Titan vanguard, wisdom against
Titan calculation, the underworld sealing what was broken, the forge building
the weapons that could kill a weather system. It took a generation. When it
ended, the Titans were not destroyed — you cannot destroy the weather, you can
only *contain* it. They were sealed: bound into vaults, sunk into trenches,
locked behind gates that must never open.

The world that rose from the Titanomachy is the Shattered Pantheon: six great
faction-grids, each a city-state built on captured Titan infrastructure, each
ruled by an Olympian intelligence that remembers the war differently. For a
long age they kept an armed peace, bound by the **Concord of Seals** — the
treaty that says no faction breaks a Titan seal to win a mortal war.

That peace is over.

---

## 2. The Previous War: The Titanomachy (backstory, not a set)

The Titanomachy is history, myth, and warning. It should be *hinted at*, never
fully shown — at least not yet. Approved references:

- **Sealed things.** Hades's vaults hold bound Titan engines. The **Abyss Gate**
  (Poseidon) and the **Leviathan Gate** seal trenches where Titan war-bodies
  sleep. The **Drowned Archive** surfaces sunken records "only when disturbed"
  — some of those records are Titan schematics.
- **Captured machines.** The **Olympian Storm Titan** is not a metaphor: it is
  a colossal weather engine built on a captured Titan core, standing "between
  Zeus and defeat." The **Keraunos** line (Keraunos Prime, Keraunos Seraph,
  Keraunos Charging Spire, Keraunos Spire) descends from Titan lightning-looms
  the forge rebuilt for Olympian hands.
- **Scars.** The **Abyssal Pressure Trench**, the **Leviathan Nursery Trench**,
  the **Worldsea Platform** — the seabed still carries Titan war-architecture,
  and Poseidon's engineers build *on* it, carefully, without waking it.
- **The Concord of Seals.** The one law all six factions still swear by. Breaking
  a seal to win the current war is the setting's ultimate taboo — and its
  ultimate Chekhov's gun.

Rule for writers: the Titans are a *pressure*, not a cast. No Titan speaks.
No Titan takes the field. Their presence is felt in sealed doors, old damage,
and the things both sides are afraid to use.

## 3. The Current War: Storm vs. Tide

With the Concord holding and the Titans sealed, the old alliance rotted into
rivalry. **Zeus** claims the sovereign's seat: the storm grid over
Neo-Olympus, the lightning looms, the sky-districts — and by extension, the
authority to rule the Pantheon as its Skyfather. **Poseidon** claims the
drowned world: the Titanomachy sank half the old earth, and Atlantis is
*rising* — the flooded arcologies, the coral archives, the abyssal trenches
are not ruins, they are a capital reclaiming its century.

Both brothers claim they are finishing the Titanomachy. Zeus says the storm
must hold the sky or the seals fail. Poseidon says the sea must take back the
land or the old wounds never close. Both are right enough to be believed, and
wrong enough to burn the world proving it.

The war is fought the way the engine plays it: **capitals fall, not armies.**
Destroy the enemy Capital and the grid collapses. Everything else — the
characters, the structures, the spells — exists to march on those six seats
of power.

### ZEUS — The Storm Grid

Fantasy: the lightning grid and conductive sky-districts of Neo-Olympus.
Fast, precise, overwhelming. (Mechanics: `docs/faction-themes.md`.)

Zeus rules from the **Cloud Throne**, a mobile palace above the mortal grid,
and speaks through the **Skyfather Archon**, his senior command intelligence.
His doctrine is the *decisive bolt*: strike first, strike from anywhere, and
let the thunder do the arguing. The **Tempest Oracle** reads combat like
weather; where she points, the **Keraunos Seraph** — a lightning-winged
executioner moving faster than prediction — is already arriving.

Key seats and engines:

- **Olympus Citadel** — the storm-crowned seat of Olympus, the capital Zeus
  inherited from the Titanomachy's end.
- **Keraunos Spire** — "a lightning conductor aimed at destiny." Part fortress,
  part weapon, part promise.
- **Celestial Throne Grid** — the highest district of Neo-Olympus, charged
  with divine authority. Taking it means taking the sky itself.
- **Worldstorm Spire** — a planetary-scale conductor dominating every visible
  skyline. The storm made architecture.
- **Storm Relay Pylon** — compact conductors redirecting atmospheric power;
  the humble, lethal backbone of the grid. Their discharges can be aimed.
- **Zeus Command Nexus** — the hardened tactical node issuing battlefield
  corrections. The war, administered.

Notable servants of the Storm:

- **Keraunos Prime** — a living thunder weapon, reserved for decisive battles.
  Deployed rarely; when it walks, the war has a deadline.
- **Eagle of the High Grid** — Zeus's sovereign hunter above the neon
  cloudline. The sky has eyes.
- **Hera Protocol Warden** — an imperial guardian enforcing Olympian command
  law. *Hera herself never takes the field* — she is the law behind the throne,
  the protocol Zeus himself must obey. Her wardens are a reminder that even
  the Skyfather answers to something.
- **Iris Signal Runner** — prism-linked couriers carrying battlefield commands
  on light itself. The grid's nervous system.
- **Stormgate Adept** — spatial technicians stepping through ionized gates.
  Distance is a suggestion.
- **Boltwing Cavalier** — mounted strikers descending with artificial
  lightning. Cavalry, reinvented at altitude.

Zeus's great spells are verdicts, not attacks: **Thunder God's Verdict**,
**Wrath of Olympus**, **Crownstorm Ascendance**. He does not fight the enemy
army. He sentences it.

### POSEIDON — The Rising Tide

Fantasy: the flooded arcologies, coral archives, abyssal trenches. Patient,
inexorable, restorative. (Mechanics: `docs/faction-themes.md`.)

Poseidon builds from the **Atlantis Nexus**, the pressure-sealed heart of the
drowned capital, and his war is the slow one: coral grows over walls, tides
grind down foundations, and what the storm breaks, the sea repairs. His
**Atlantis Tide Sovereign** stands armored in living abyssal crystal; his
**Kraken Prime Avatar** is the largest autonomous war-body the ocean god
fields. But his most feared servant is the **Abysswalker Nereid** — "an
impossible infiltrator moving through water, stone, and folded space."
If she is behind you, the sea was already there.

Key seats and engines:

- **Atlantis Nexus** — the pressure-sealed heart of Atlantis. The capital that
  drowned and refused to die.
- **Trident Bastion** — three sea walls joined as one fortress.
- **Abyssal Court** — a throne hall beneath all sunlight. Poseidon's deepest
  seat, and his darkest.
- **Atlantis Crown Basin** — the drowned capital's central reservoir, risen
  into war.
- **Leviathan Nursery Trench** — a deep biome where siege organisms mature
  unseen. Patience, weaponized.
- **Leviathan Gate** — a living fortress sealing the road to Atlantis. It is
  also, quietly, one of the Titan seals. Poseidon garrisons it heavily and
  does not discuss why.
- **Coral Data Reef** — a living archive grown through synthetic coral. The
  sea remembers everything; the reef is how it files it.
- **Tidal Pump Station** — the pressure engine sustaining the flooded grid.
  Humble, vital, and very hard to kill.
- **Worldsea Platform** — a mobile oceanic district supporting a complete
  battle line. Atlantis does not march. It *drifts* into position.

Notable servants of the Tide:

- **Leviathan Wakeborn** — a massive bioframe rising in the wake of old sea
  gods. The past, given a hull.
- **Poseidon's Trident Core** — an autonomous weapon carrying a shard of
  oceanic authority. A piece of the god, delegated.
- **Naiad Flowshaper** — a liquid-motion engineer who bends battlefield
  routes. The map is negotiable.
- **Delphic Sonar Adept** — ranged operators reading enemies through
  reflected sound. Nothing approaches unheard.
- **Undertow Stalker** — an amphibious hunter waiting beneath flooded
  terrain. The water is never empty.
- **Abyssal Molecrab** — a plated excavator concealed beneath seabed
  holdings. The floor is also a door.

Poseidon's great spells are the sea's patience made sudden: **Maelstrom
Verdict**, **Crushing Depths**, **Erode Foundation** — and his mercy,
**Restorative Tide**, which no other faction can cheaply match. He heals what
he holds. He simply holds more of the world every year.

## 4. The Four Unaligned — Courted by Both Sides

Four major factions have not joined the Storm-Tide war. Both Zeus and
Poseidon court them openly — with embassies, bribes, threats, and promises —
because the war's arithmetic changes the moment any one of them moves. Each
has its own reason to wait, and its own price.

### ARES — War (the proving grounds)

Fantasy: war cults, crimson training yards, endless conflict. The faction of
attack buffs, expendable attackers, and self-sacrifice — it pays HP and burns
its own permanents to deal damage.

Ares is the war's *beneficiary*. Every battle fought with his doctrines
proves his creed: that conflict is the only honest god. Zeus offers him the
vanguard of the storm — first strike, first glory. Poseidon offers him the
grinding war of attrition his cults were built for. Ares takes meetings with
both, accepts gifts from both, and commits to neither, because **a god of war
does not end wars**. His price is rumored to be simple: whichever side
promises the war never ends gets his legions. Neither side will say it aloud.
Both sides have drafted the speech.

Story role: the arms both sides want pointed at the enemy, and the one
faction that might prefer the war to *continue* rather than be won.

### ATHENA — Discipline (the academies)

Fantasy: academies, data courts, the Aegis. Measured, mutually supporting —
formations, tutoring, information advantage. The faction that wins by knowing
more and standing together.

Athena has declared herself **arbiter**. Her data courts recognize neither
brother's claim to the sovereign seat until one proves legitimate rule — and
her definition of legitimacy is a curriculum neither war plan survives. Zeus
courts her for *legitimacy*: an Athenian charter would make his Skyfather
claim law. Poseidon courts her for *adjudication*: he wants the courts to
rule the drowned lands his by right of reclamation.

She answers both with the same price: **submit the war to arbitration, and
she will choose the Pantheon's ruler.** Neither brother will. So her owlwatch
towers observe, her academies drill, and her phalanxes wait in perfect
formation — the most disciplined army on the board, pointed at no one, which
is exactly what makes everyone nervous.

Story role: the judge neither side can bribe and neither dares to fight.

### HADES — The Underworld (the sealed vault)

Fantasy: Styx conduits, memory towers, the sealed vault. Nothing stays
buried. The faction of death triggers and recursion — it profits from its own
permanents dying, and one day its dead will come back.

Hades is not courted. Hades is *consulted*, carefully, the way one handles a
sealed door. He is the warden of the Concord of Seals — the vaults beneath
his realm hold bound Titan engines, and his silence is the only proof they
are still bound. Zeus sends embassies asking for assurance; Poseidon sends
divers asking for maps. Hades answers both in the same coin: **debts.** The
dead of the Storm-Tide war are already arriving on his shores, and he keeps
immaculate ledgers. When the war ends, both brothers will owe him — and he
collects in whatever currency he chooses.

Both sides fear the same rumor: that Hades could *open* a seal and end the
war in an afternoon, and that his price for doing so would be the winner's
soul. Nobody has asked him directly. Nobody will.

Story role: the banker of the dead, the keeper of the seals, the faction
whose neutrality is the only thing keeping the war mortal.

### HEPHAESTUS — The Forge (the cinderworks)

Fantasy: foundries, assembly lines, automaton legions. Build the machine,
then turn it on. The faction of structures that improve the longer they run
and engines that convert resources into anything.

Hephaestus is the war's *merchant*. His assembly lines run day and night,
and both sides' requisitions are stamped APPROVED — at prices that rise with
the casualty lists. Zeus's spires and Poseidon's pump stations alike were
built in his cinderworks. His official position is perfect neutrality: the
forge serves whoever pays, and the forge always gets paid.

Unofficially, the forge keeps *one* ledger that matters: which side's
machines perform better in the field. The **Talos** line — his ancient
automaton war-bodies, first built for the Titanomachy — has not been offered
to either side. When Hephaestus sells the Talos line, the war ends the next
season. Everyone knows it. He is waiting to see which brother's grid deserves
to inherit the machines that killed the Titans.

Story role: the arms dealer both sides depend on, whose true allegiance is
to the machines themselves.

## 5. Dramatis Personae (from the cards)

Figures who may be named in flavor text, campaigns, and trailers. Do not
promote unnamed card characters to this list without a story reason.

- **Zeus** — the Skyfather. Claims the sovereign seat; rules the storm grid
  from the Cloud Throne. Never depicted as a unit — he is the faction.
- **The Skyfather Archon** — Zeus's senior command intelligence; his voice
  on the battlefield.
- **Hera** — never takes the field. She is the Protocol: Olympian command
  law, enforced by her Wardens. Even Zeus answers to her. (A power, not a
  faction — do not make her a seventh faction.)
- **Poseidon** — the Ocean God. Claims the drowned world; rules from the
  Atlantis Nexus. Never depicted as a unit — he is the faction.
- **The Atlantis Tide Sovereign** — Poseidon's royal guardian, armored in
  living abyssal crystal.
- **Keraunos Prime / Keraunos Seraph** — Zeus's living thunder weapons;
  reserved for decisive battles.
- **The Tempest Oracle / Oracle of Storms** — predictive intelligences
  reading combat like weather.
- **The Kraken Prime Avatar** — Poseidon's largest autonomous war-body.
- **The Abysswalker Nereid** — the infiltrator who moves through water,
  stone, and folded space. Both sides' intelligence services are obsessed
  with her.
- **The Leviathan Wakeborn** — the past, given a hull.

## 6. Story Hooks (approved future threads)

These are load-bearing mysteries. Future sets, DLC factions, and campaigns
may develop them; nothing may resolve them without a design review.

1. **The seals are under strain.** The war's escalating energies disturb the
   old bindings. Hades's ledgers show anomalies. (Titan hook.)
2. **Hera's Protocol has a contingency.** The Wardens enforce command law —
   but whose commands, if Zeus falls? (Zeus-court hook.)
3. **The Talos line is still unsold.** Hephaestus watches the field data.
   (Forge hook.)
4. **Athena's arbitration stands open.** The price of her entry is the war's
   end by judgment, not conquest. (Discipline hook.)
5. **Ares's price is the war itself.** Someone will eventually say it aloud.
   (War hook.)
6. **The Drowned Archive keeps surfacing.** Sunken records, Titan schematics,
   things best left filed. (Tide hook.)
7. **Four factions, four DLCs.** Ares, Athena, Hades, and Hephaestus each
   enter the game as their story breaks — their arrival should feel like a
   *plot event*, not a content drop.

---

## 7. Consistency Rules for Future Cards

1. **Names carry history.** New cards should reference the world: districts,
   engines, offices, and veterans of the war. A name with no story is a
   missed opportunity.
2. **Descriptions are canon.** The `description` field on every card is
   in-world text. It must not contradict this bible.
3. **The six stay six.** No seventh faction, no Hera faction, no Titan
   faction — unless this document is revised by design review.
4. **The Titans stay sealed.** No Titan units, no Titan POV, no opened seals
   — until the story says otherwise, loudly and deliberately.
5. **Tech, not marble.** Neo-futurist Greco-tech in every brief: data
   streams, pressure seals, command protocols, bioframes. Togas never.
6. **Capitals are the war.** Every faction's story bends toward its seats
   of power. Destroy the Capital, end the grid.
7. **The four unaligned have prices.** Any story in which Ares, Athena,
   Hades, or Hephaestus joins the war must name the price they were paid —
   and it must match section 4.
